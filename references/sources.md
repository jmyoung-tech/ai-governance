## Canada, federal

### Status note
Canada has no in-force comprehensive federal AI statute. The Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA), tabled as part of Bill C-27, died on the Order Paper when Parliament was prorogued on January 6, 2025. In June 2025, the Minister of AI and Digital Innovation (Evan Solomon) confirmed AIDA will not return as drafted, and that a new standalone AI bill is anticipated. On September 26, 2025, the federal government launched the AI Strategy Task Force and a 30-day national sprint to inform that legislation. Until federal legislation lands, AI governance in Canada is shaped by (a) sectoral regulator guidance (especially OSFI for FRFIs), (b) federal and provincial privacy law, (c) provincial public-sector statutes (notably Ontario Bill 194), and (d) voluntary codes and TBS directives for federal institutions.

### Government and lead bodies
- [Minister of AI and Digital Innovation announcements (ISED)](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news.html), federal AI policy announcements
- [AI Strategy Task Force (Sept 26, 2025 launch)](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2025/09/government-of-canada-launches-ai-strategy-task-force-and-public-engagement-on-the-development-of-the-next-ai-strategy.html), Task Force composition, mandate, national sprint inputs
- [Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED), AI page](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/innovation-better-canada/en/artificial-intelligence-and-data), historical AIDA materials and forward-looking AI policy
- [Voluntary Code of Conduct on the Responsible Development and Management of Advanced Generative AI Systems (Sept 2023)](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/ised/en/voluntary-code-conduct-responsible-development-and-management-advanced-generative-ai-systems), still operative as soft law for signatories
- [Treasury Board Directive on Automated Decision-Making](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=32592), binding on federal institutions; sets Algorithmic Impact Assessment requirements
- [AI Strategy for the Federal Public Service 2025-2027](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/responsible-use-ai/gc-ai-strategy-overview.html), TBS strategy for AI adoption in federal operations
- [Guide on the Use of Generative AI (TBS)](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/responsible-use-ai/guide-use-generative-ai.html), practical guidance for federal employees
- [Algorithmic Impact Assessment Tool](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/responsible-use-ai/algorithmic-impact-assessment.html), required under the TBS Directive on ADM

### Privacy regulators (federal)
- [Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC), AI hub](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/), primary federal privacy regulator's AI work
- [OPC, Principles for responsible, trustworthy and privacy-protective generative AI technologies (Dec 7, 2023)](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/), joint statement with all provincial and territorial commissioners; interprets existing privacy law for generative AI
- [OPC, leadership on AI and privacy](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/leadership-ai/), international joint statements (G7 DPAs, GPA) the OPC has co-signed
- [OPC, AI Scribes guidance (Jan 28, 2026)](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/), sector-specific guidance for AI scribes in health care; useful as a model for sectoral OPC guidance
- [OPC 2024-2025 Annual Report](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/ar_index/202425/ar_202425/), Commissioner's stated priorities including AI

## Canada, financial services regulators

This subsection matters disproportionately for FRFIs and PSPs; OSFI E-23 is the most consequential Canadian AI-specific regulatory instrument currently scheduled to take effect.

### OSFI (Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions)
- [OSFI, Guideline E-23 Model Risk Management (2027)](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/guideline-e-23-model-risk-management-2027), final guideline published Sept 11, 2025; effective May 1, 2027; expressly covers AI/ML models; applies to all FRFIs (banks, foreign bank branches, life and P&C insurers, trust and loan companies)
- [OSFI, Guideline B-13 Technology and Cyber Risk Management](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/technology-cyber-risk-management), adjacent obligations for AI as a technology asset
- [OSFI, Guideline B-10 Third-Party Risk Management](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/guidance/guidance-library/third-party-risk-management-guideline), TPRM obligations that capture AI vendors
- [Financial Industry Forum on Artificial Intelligence (FIFAI)](https://www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca/en/data-forms/financial-industry-forum-artificial-intelligence), OSFI / Finance Canada / Global Risk Institute series; second cycle began May 2025; published risk reports are important reference material

### FCAC (Financial Consumer Agency of Canada)
- [FCAC home](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency.html), consumer-protection mandate over federally regulated banks; relevant where AI touches consumer-facing decisions, complaints handling, and marketing
- [FCAC supervisory framework](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/programs/supervision.html), how FCAC monitors compliance with market-conduct obligations

### FINTRAC (Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre)
- [FINTRAC home](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/intro-eng), Canada's FIU; relevant for AI used in AML/ATF (transaction monitoring, sanctions and PEP screening, identity verification)
- [FINTRAC guidance](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/guidance-directives/1-eng), operational guidance under the PCMLTFA; the regulator does not currently issue AI-specific rules but AI-driven processes must satisfy underlying PCMLTFA obligations
- [FINTRAC Annual Report 2024-2025](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/publications/ar/2025/ar2025-eng), describes FINTRAC's own AI/ML use and supervisory priorities

### Payments Canada
- [Payments Canada, home](https://www.payments.ca/), operator of Lynx, ACSS, and the forthcoming Real-Time Rail (RTR); rules and standards apply to all member FIs and (via PSP participation) to AI-touched payments workflows
- [Payments Canada, RTR Participation Guide for PSPs](https://www.payments.ca/), eligibility, technical, operational, and business requirements; relevant for any AI in onboarding, fraud, or message-processing in RTR-connected workflows
- [Payments Canada, Modernization](https://www.payments.ca/about-us/our-work-payments-modernization), RTR program status and ISO 20022 transition

### Bank of Canada (retail payments supervision and AI research)
- [Bank of Canada, Retail Payments Supervision](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/core-functions/financial-system/retail-payments-supervision/), BoC's role under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA); approximately 1,500 PSPs registered
- [Bank of Canada, Money and Payments research](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/research/money-and-payments/), staff research including AI applications in central banking and payments oversight
- [Bank of Canada 2025-2027 Strategic Plan, Focusing our innovation](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/about/governance-documents/the-bank-of-canadas-2025-27-strategic-plan/focusing-our-innovation/), explicit commitment to responsible AI integration

### Provincial financial regulators
- [AMF (Quebec), Model Risk Management Guideline (June 2025)](https://lautorite.qc.ca/en), provincial counterpart to OSFI E-23 for Quebec-licensed insurers, deposit institutions, and financial services cooperatives; largely aligned with E-23
- [FSRA (Ontario)](https://www.fsrao.ca/), Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario; relevant for provincially regulated insurers, credit unions, mortgage brokers
- [BCFSA (British Columbia)](https://www.bcfsa.ca/), provincial regulator for BC credit unions, insurers, trust companies, mortgage brokers

## Canada, provinces and territories

### Privacy commissioners (AI-relevant)
- [Information and Privacy Commissioner of Ontario (IPC)](https://www.ipc.on.ca/), active on AI guidance and Bill 194 oversight
- [Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec (CAI)](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/), enforces Quebec Law 25, which expressly addresses automated decision-making in the private sector
- [Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner of Alberta (OIPC)](https://oipc.ab.ca/), published August 2025 report recommending Alberta create its own AI framework aligned with the EU AI Act
- [Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for BC](https://www.oipc.bc.ca/), joint signatory to OPC generative AI Principles
- The remaining provincial and territorial commissioners all co-signed the December 2023 OPC Principles

### Provincial AI legislation and policy
- [Ontario, Bill 194 (Enhancing Digital Security and Trust Act, 2024)](https://www.ola.org/en/legislative-business/bills/parliament-43/session-1/bill-194), Royal Assent November 25, 2024; binds Ontario public sector entities, school boards, and children's aid societies regarding AI use and cybersecurity; substantive rules left to regulations
- [Quebec, Law 25 (private sector)](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/), automated decision-making transparency and rights, including a right to obtain information about how a decision was made and the personal information used
- [British Columbia, Policy on the use of generative AI](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/) and BC Digital Code of Practice, public-sector guidance
- [Saskatchewan, Generative AI guidelines for government employees](https://www.saskatchewan.ca/), public-sector guidance

### Provincial trackers and analysis
- The IPC, CAI, and OIPC AB publish regular AI-related guidance and submissions; their submissions on Bill 194 and Bill C-27 are useful primary sources on Canadian regulator thinking

## Adding new sources

When adding a source, include:
- A short descriptor (what makes it useful, what it covers)
- The URL

Prefer primary sources (regulators, official documents) over secondary commentary when both are available. For Canada, prefer the regulator's own page over law-firm summaries.
